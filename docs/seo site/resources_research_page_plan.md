# Tellann Research Page — Complete SEO Marketing Specification

TELLANN · RESOURCES · RESEARCH

# Research that helps teams understand software behavior.

Route: `/research` · Page type: Public research and insights hub · Primary objective: Credibility, education, evidence-based thought leadership, and qualified product discovery

## 1. What the Research page should accomplish

The `/research` page should position Tellann as a company investigating important questions about software quality, behavioral testing, workflow validation, and the evidence teams use to decide whether their applications are working as intended.

Unlike the Blog, which publishes engineering commentary and perspectives, the Research page should emphasize methods, evidence, findings, limitations, and reproducible insights.

Its core question is:

What can we actually demonstrate about software behavior, and what does that evidence mean for the teams building it?

The most important distinction is between Tellann's own product philosophy and independently established research findings. Tellann's architecture is built around reconciling declared intent with observed behavior; that is an appropriate research focus. It is not, by itself, proof that Tellann improves defect detection, reduces QA costs, or increases release reliability by a measurable amount.

Those are claims to investigate, not conclusions to publish prematurely.

The Research page should feel more authoritative and methodical than the Blog, but still accessible to developers, QA engineers, product teams, managers, and founders.

## 2. Primary target audiences

Each user class approaches research with a different question.

| Audience                             | Primary research interest                      | What they should gain                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Software developers                  | Application states, paths, debugging evidence  | Better understanding of what workflow observations can establish    |
| QA engineers                         | Coverage, test adequacy, missing paths         | Methods for evaluating what testing has and has not demonstrated    |
| Engineering managers                 | Validation confidence, release evidence, risk  | A clearer framework for interpreting quality reports                |
| Product managers                     | Requirements versus implemented behavior       | Ways to evaluate whether actual workflows reflect intended outcomes |
| Startup founders                     | Practical testing tradeoffs, reliability, cost | Guidance on prioritizing quality work with limited resources        |

Research should serve technical evaluators without making everyone else read academic papers to understand a basic concept.

## 11. Section 07 — How Tellann Conducts Research

### Heading

> From a question to a defensible conclusion.

Visitors should understand how Tellann distinguishes hypotheses from findings.

Present the research process as a six-step methodology:

1. Define the research question

   Specify the behavior, testing technique, or quality signal being investigated.
2. Establish a baseline

   Identify the applications, workflows, requirements, or conventional measurements used for comparison.
3. Design a reproducible experiment

   Document the test environment, datasets, instrumentation, and evaluation procedures.
4. Collect and validate evidence

   Capture relevant observations, inspect data quality, and identify excluded or inconclusive results.
5. Analyze findings

   Explain measured differences, uncertainty, threats to validity, and what cannot be concluded.
6. Publish methods and limitations

   Release sufficient methodological details to help readers evaluate the work.

This describes the recommended research standard Tellann should adopt, not a claim that it has already completed studies using this process.

### Visual design

Desktop: horizontal six-stage research pipeline or two-row methodology diagram.

- SVG master: 1800×650px.
- Rendered: \~1120×405px.
- Mobile: vertical stage list.
- Icon master: 96×96px per stage.
- Icon display: 40–48px.

Animate a connecting line over 1.5–2 seconds once the diagram enters view. Each stage can become highlighted in sequence.

Avoid making the diagram's meaning dependent on the animation. Labels and descriptions should always be visible.

## 16. Section 12 — Research in Practice

### Heading

> From research questions to practical engineering decisions.

This section connects research concepts with the types of problems Tellann's target customers encounter.

It should not be formatted as customer case studies unless there are real customers and documented outcomes.

### Scenario A — Developer

Question: A checkout test passed, but which workflow states were actually demonstrated?

Explain how declaring expected states and examining observed evidence could make the coverage discussion more precise.

Related product area: Flow Declaration and Behavior Graph.

### Scenario B — QA engineer

Question: A test suite executes successfully, but important alternate paths remain unexplored.

Explain how a QA engineer can distinguish demonstrated states from uncovered scenarios and prioritize additional verification.

Related product area: Reconciliation and QA Reports.

### Scenario C — Engineering manager

Question: A release report says 85% coverage. What does that number actually mean?

Explain denominator definitions, evidence scope, and why aggregate metrics should be accompanied by concrete unverified workflows.

Do not present 85% as a Tellann benchmark or actual customer result.

Related product area: QA Reports.

### Scenario D — Product manager

Question: A feature is implemented, but the product requirements describe additional behavior.

Show how expressed intent can be compared against demonstrated application execution.

Related product area: Flow Declaration and Reconciliation.

- Current conceptual approach.
- Known limitations.
- Research status.
- Links to related publications when available.

No images necessary. This should be deliberately text-forward.

Future ideas such as autonomous testing and live production monitoring must remain labeled as future research or roadmap directions unless shipping status changes.

## 18. Section 14 — Research Integrity and Limitations

### Heading

> Evidence matters. So do its limits.

This is a trust-building section, not legal boilerplate.

It should explicitly explain:

- Research claims are supported by described observations or citations.
- Product-generated examples are not automatically research findings.
- Demonstration-only evidence does not describe all possible application behavior.
- Unobserved behavior is not necessarily defective.
- Results may not generalize to every application architecture.
- Benchmarks need representative datasets and transparent evaluation methods.
- Comparisons involving Tellann should disclose that they are company-conducted research.
- Findings should distinguish hypotheses, preliminary observations, and validated results.

### Privacy statement

Research artifacts must not disclose customer source code, confidential application data, user sessions, credentials, or personally identifiable information without lawful authorization and suitable protections.

Where research involves Tellann's desktop demonstrations, describe local code handling and explicit instrumentation approval consistently with the published security architecture. Do not imply the desktop agent uploads all local source code to conduct research.

### Design

Full-width text section with a narrow 760–900px content column, numbered principles, and links to `/security`, `/privacy`, and methodology documentation.

Use no stock photography here. Credibility comes from clarity, not decoration.

## 24. Individual research publication template

A genuine publication page should have:

1. Breadcrumbs and publication type.
2. Title, authors, institutional affiliations where appropriate, and status.
3. Publication date, version, and revision history.
4. Abstract in plain language.
5. Key findings, or a clear explanation that results are pending.
6. Research question and hypothesis.
7. Methodology and experimental setup.
8. Dataset description and eligibility criteria.
9. Results and figures.
10. Interpretation, limitations, and threats to validity.
11. Conclusions and practical implications.
12. References, citations, and research artifacts.
13. Related publications and relevant Tellann product links.

Recommended layout:

- Main content: 720–780px.
- Sticky section navigation: 220–260px.
- Research figures: up to 1000px wide.
- Tables: responsive with explicit headings and units.
- H1: 42–56px.
- Body text: 17–18px.

Give research publications stronger source and methodology visibility than blog posts. Readers should be able to determine where a claim came from.

## 32. Final positioning recommendation

For Tellann, `/research` can eventually become one of the most important credibility-building pages on the marketing website.

However, it should launch as a research-directions and methodology hub, not as a fabricated research institute or benchmark library.

The priority is to help visitors understand the distinction between declared intent, observed behavior, and supported conclusions. Once Tellann has empirical evaluations, those findings can become the centerpiece of the page.

That creates a sustainable progression:

Research questions → Transparent methods → Real findings → Practical implications → Product understanding.

It gives developers and QA engineers technical depth, engineering managers and product teams clarity, and founders a credible reason to trust the company's approach—without overstating what the research has established.
